# Brief on Project to Test the Hypothesis in the *2028 Global Intelligence Crisis* Paper

## The Problem

The *2028 Global Intelligence Crisis* presents a scenario in which advances in agentic AI allow companies to build more software internally, reducing their reliance on commercial software-as-a-service platforms. The paper uses ServiceNow as an example, suggesting that its net-new annual contract value growth could decelerate as customers reconsider, reduce, or replace purchased workflow software.

Because the scenario is written from a future perspective, its statements can be mistaken for observations of current business conditions. A reported slowdown in ServiceNow growth would not, by itself, validate the paper's argument. Growth could also slow because of normal seasonality, macroeconomic conditions, longer procurement cycles, pricing changes, competitive pressure, or unusually difficult comparisons with prior periods.

This project will investigate whether current evidence supports the paper's proposed causal mechanism:

> Enterprises are using AI to build or automate workflow capabilities internally, causing them to purchase, renew, or expand less commercial SaaS software.

The investigation will focus initially on ServiceNow, with relevant comparisons to companies such as Salesforce, Atlassian, Workday, Oracle, and SAP. Looking across multiple companies will help distinguish a ServiceNow-specific issue from a broader change in enterprise software demand.

## Hypothesis

### Primary Hypothesis

Rapid adoption of generative and agentic AI is enabling enterprises to build or automate workflow capabilities internally, reducing demand for commercial SaaS platforms and contributing to measurable deceleration in ServiceNow's growth.

### Expected Evidence if the Hypothesis Is Correct

The evidence should show more than slower headline revenue growth. It should reveal a consistent connection between AI adoption and reduced SaaS purchasing, including several of the following:

- Declining or persistently decelerating subscription revenue, current remaining performance obligations, net-new annual contract value, or comparable forward-looking indicators.
- Management commentary describing contract reductions, slower expansions, lower seat growth, longer sales cycles, or increased customer scrutiny of software spending.
- Customers stating that internally developed AI agents or workflow applications are replacing, reducing, or avoiding purchases of ServiceNow capabilities.
- Systems integrators and implementation partners reporting weaker pipelines, smaller deployments, reduced implementation demand, or customers redirecting spending from SaaS implementations to internal AI development.
- Similar demand changes appearing across other enterprise software companies, indicating a broader structural shift rather than a company-specific problem.
- Evidence that internal AI solutions have moved beyond demonstrations and are operating reliably in production at sufficient scale to replace paid software capabilities.

### Alternative Explanations to Evaluate

The investigation must also test whether any observed deceleration is better explained by:

- Macroeconomic uncertainty or general reductions in enterprise technology spending.
- Normal quarterly seasonality.
- Difficult comparisons with unusually strong prior periods.
- Longer enterprise procurement and approval cycles.
- Changes in pricing, packaging, or contract structure.
- Competition from another commercial platform rather than internal AI development.
- Execution, product, or sales issues specific to ServiceNow.
- Customers consolidating vendors while continuing to purchase commercial SaaS products.

## How I Would Know I Was Wrong

The hypothesis would be weakened or rejected if the evidence shows that:

- ServiceNow maintains stable or improving forward-looking growth indicators, including current remaining performance obligations, renewal activity, customer expansion, and large-deal activity.
- Customers adopt AI primarily through ServiceNow products rather than using AI to replace the platform.
- AI features increase platform usage, expand contracts, or create additional demand for workflow automation.
- Customers build AI prototypes internally but continue relying on commercial platforms for security, governance, integration, reliability, regulatory compliance, and enterprise support.
- Reported growth deceleration is temporary and is better explained by seasonality, macroeconomic conditions, procurement timing, pricing changes, or company-specific execution.
- Competing enterprise software providers do not exhibit similar demand patterns.
- Customers shift spending from ServiceNow to another commercial vendor rather than replacing purchased software with internally developed AI.
- There are isolated examples of AI replacing individual features, but no evidence that these examples materially affect renewals, expansions, contract values, or platform-level demand.
- Management, customer, and partner evidence consistently indicates that AI is complementary to enterprise SaaS rather than a substitute for it.

## Evidence Standard

A single earnings miss, stock-price decline, customer anecdote, or management comment will not be considered sufficient evidence.

The hypothesis should be considered supported only when evidence establishes the following chain:

1. Enterprises deploy internally developed AI capabilities in production.
2. Those capabilities replace or materially reduce the need for commercial SaaS functionality.
3. Customers consequently reduce, avoid, or decline to expand commercial software contracts.
4. The behavior is visible in reported operating metrics, customer disclosures, or credible partner and market evidence.
5. The pattern appears across multiple customers or providers rather than as an isolated case.

## Measures to Track

### Company Performance

- Subscription revenue growth
- Current remaining performance obligations growth
- Total remaining performance obligations
- Net-new annual contract value, when disclosed
- Renewal and expansion commentary
- Number and value of large contracts, when disclosed
- Management guidance and revisions
- Sales-cycle and customer-budget commentary

### Customer Behavior

- Contract reductions or platform consolidation
- Decisions not to renew or expand
- Replacement of purchased workflows with internal applications or agents
- Movement of spending from SaaS licenses to AI infrastructure and development
- Production deployments that replace existing commercial functionality

### Partner and Competitor Signals

- Implementation and consulting pipeline commentary
- Demand reported by major systems integrators
- Comparable growth patterns among other enterprise software providers
- Changes in enterprise software hiring and implementation roles
- Announcements of internally developed AI workflow platforms

## Evaluation Approach

Each new piece of evidence will be classified as:

- **Supports the hypothesis**
- **Contradicts the hypothesis**
- **Supports an alternative explanation**
- **Inconclusive**

The assessment will distinguish between:

- **Observed fact:** A reported metric, customer decision, or direct statement.
- **Interpretation:** What the evidence may indicate.
- **Causal evidence:** Proof that internal AI adoption influenced a purchasing decision.
- **Scenario claim:** A projected outcome from the paper that has not yet occurred or been independently verified.

## Decision Rule

The hypothesis will not be considered validated merely because ServiceNow growth slows.

It will be considered increasingly credible only if slower growth occurs alongside repeated, credible evidence that customers are reducing commercial SaaS purchases specifically because internally developed AI provides an adequate substitute.

Conversely, if ServiceNow continues to grow while customers adopt its AI capabilities, or if any slowdown is better explained by unrelated economic or company-specific factors, the paper's proposed mechanism will be considered unsupported.
