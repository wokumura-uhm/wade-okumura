# BUS-620 Perfect Competition Brief

The Problem: The objective is to determine the profit-maximizing allocation of 64 beds among tomatoes, carrots, and mesclun while remaining within available labor and budget constraints. The planting decision must be made at the beginning of the season and cannot be changed during the growing cycle. The resources consist of 64 beds (16 beds by 4 plots), one pair of hands (the farmer), and the budget for up to 4 temporary workers for the season($25,000 per worker calculated at 1,440 hours each at $17.36 per hour). The plan must be committed for the entire season. The fixed costs for the season are $20,000 for the beds and $50,000 for the farmer (720 field hours at $34.72 per hour). The below table shows the known facts.

| Crop | Max beds | Price $ per bed | Labor hours per week per bed | Fertilizer $ per bed | Diminishing returns |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tomatoes | 20 | 8,800 | 2.50 | 880 | 10.00% per bed |
| Carrots | 20 | 2,094 | 0.833 | 440 | 2.50% per bed |
| Mesclun | 30 | 2,700 | 1.25 | 880 | 1.25% per bed |

Hypothesis: Because carrots require the fewest labor hours per bed, have the lowest fertilizer costs, and experience relatively small diminishing returns, a larger share of the available beds may be allocated to carrots. Conversely, tomatoes have the highest labor requirements, fertilizer costs, and diminishing returns, which may limit the optimal number of tomato beds despite their higher revenue potential. Therefore, the optimal planting plan is expected to favor carrots over tomatoes, subject to profitability and labor constraints.
