## Working Title

Has the Bank of Japan Regained Control of Japanese Interest Rates?

## Research Question

Has the Bank of Japan regained control of Japanese interest rates, or do Federal Reserve policy decisions continue to drive Japanese bond yields and the USD/JPY exchange rate?

## Working Argument

Although the Bank of Japan has begun normalizing monetary policy after decades of ultra-low interest rates, Japanese financial markets remain highly connected to U.S. capital markets.

Because Japanese institutions hold significant amounts of U.S. Treasury securities and actively allocate capital globally, changes in Federal Reserve policy may continue to exert greater influence on Japanese yields and the yen than domestic monetary policy actions.

If this relationship remains strong, Japan's monetary independence may be more limited than commonly assumed.

## Why This Matters

Japan operates one of the world's largest sovereign debt markets while also holding substantial U.S. Treasury investments.

If Japanese borrowing costs remain primarily influenced by U.S. monetary conditions, then:

- Bank of Japan policy effectiveness may be constrained.
- Japanese government financing costs may be sensitive to foreign policy decisions.
- Exchange-rate volatility may be driven more by interest-rate differentials than domestic fundamentals.
- Future BOJ normalization efforts may have limited impact.

## Proposed Mechanisms

### Interest Rate Differentials

Investors compare returns across countries.

When U.S. yields increase relative to Japanese yields, capital may flow toward U.S. assets, increasing demand for dollars and reducing demand for yen.

### Treasury Market Transmission

Japanese institutions hold significant U.S. Treasury securities.

Changes in Treasury yields may influence portfolio allocations and Japanese bond market pricing.

### Exchange Rate Effects

A wider U.S.-Japan interest-rate differential may:

- Strengthen the U.S. dollar
- Weaken the yen
- Place upward pressure on Japanese yields

Analysis window:

## Preliminary Expectations

If the hypothesis is correct:

- JGB yields will react more strongly to Federal Reserve actions than BOJ actions.
- USD/JPY will react more strongly to Federal Reserve actions than BOJ actions.
- Changes in U.S. Treasury yields will precede or accompany major changes in Japanese yields.
- Interest-rate differentials will explain a significant portion of USD/JPY movement.

## What Would Change My Mind

The hypothesis would be weakened if:

- BOJ actions consistently produce larger movements in JGB yields than Federal Reserve actions.
- Japanese yields move independently of Treasury yields.
- USD/JPY responds more strongly to BOJ policy than Federal Reserve policy.
- Evidence suggests Japan has become less dependent on U.S. monetary conditions following policy normalization.
